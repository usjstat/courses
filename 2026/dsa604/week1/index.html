Course overview

COURSE OVERVIEW

From Bayesian reasoning to computational modelling

Bayesian inference combines information about unknown quantities with evidence from observed data. This course develops the statistical reasoning behind that process and the computational methods needed when posterior distributions cannot be evaluated analytically.

Students first formulate a model and interpret its components. They then use R to simulate from posterior distributions, diagnose computation, and assess model predictions. Selected comparisons with Python/PyMC support the same modelling workflow.

What we will study

  • Bayesian probability and statistical reasoning
  • Prior distributions, likelihoods, and posterior inference
  • Posterior summaries, simulation, and Monte Carlo methods
  • Markov chain Monte Carlo and convergence diagnostics
  • Bayesian regression and generalized Bayesian models
  • Hierarchical models and partial pooling
  • Posterior predictive checking and model comparison
  • Predictive assessment and the Bayesian modelling workflow

Computing environment

Primary language: R

Selected comparisons: Python/PyMC

Core R tools include tidyverse, bayesrules, rstanarm, cmdstanr, posterior, bayesplot, tidybayes, and loo.

Learning approach. Lectures combine statistical explanations and guided computation. Practical sessions provide opportunities to implement models, examine posterior output, and interpret results.

Learning objectives

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

What this course develops

1

Specify Bayesian models using prior distributions, likelihood functions, and posterior distributions.

2

Use numerical simulation and MCMC to obtain and summarize posterior distributions.

3

Fit and interpret Bayesian regression, generalized, and hierarchical models using R.

4

Examine computational diagnostics and evaluate models through posterior predictive checks and predictive assessment.
